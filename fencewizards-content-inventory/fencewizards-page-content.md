## Construction fencing questions

### How much lead time do you need on a construction site?

Twenty four to forty eight hours is the normal window and it covers most jobs. We do take emergency work faster than that. The more notice we have, the more of the install we can plan rather than improvise, which usually means a cleaner line and fewer changes later.

### Do you charge per month once the fence is up?

No. The rental is a flat fee, the standard term runs up to twelve months, and short jobs of a few months or less get a discount. If your project runs long, the number does not change. There is no charge to come and collect the fence at the end.

### What happens when the site plan changes and the fence is in the way?

You call and we come out. Fencing is in somebody's way at least once on every project, so we built the business around answering that call rather than around avoiding it. General contractors get same day service on moves, gate relocations and sections added to an existing run.

### Can you fence a site where posts cannot be driven?

Yes. Panels sit in stands weighted with sandbags and need no ground penetration at all, which is the usual answer on a paved lot, a deck, or a site with utilities close to the surface. ### Do you offer net thirty terms to contractors?

Net thirty is normal for clients we have worked with before. Smaller or unvetted accounts pay up front, and we say that on the first call rather than at the invoice. Certificates of insurance for your vendor file are no problem: umbrella, general liability, commercial auto and workers compensation.

### Will you add gates after the fence is already up?

Yes. Pedestrian gates and drive gates can be set into an existing run. It is worth telling us where deliveries come in before the install, because gates in the right places save your crew time every day, but adding one later is a normal call for us to take.

## What Are Temporary Fence Panels?

Temporary fence panels are freestanding, modular fence sections that stand on their own with the help of weighted bases, typically sandbags, rather than posts driven into the ground. ## How the Panel-and-Stand System Works

Each panel slots into a pair of rubber or plastic feet, and sandbags are placed over those feet to keep everything stable under normal wind and foot-traffic conditions. The setup does not require tools in most configurations, panels clip or pin together to form a continuous barrier, and the sandbag weight does the job concrete footings would do on a permanent fence.

The practical advantage is flexibility. If a contractor needs to shift the fence line mid-project to allow equipment access, a crew member can move panels in minutes. If an event organizer needs to widen a pedestrian entry, they simply uncouple a section. - Utility and infrastructure work: Protect excavation zones, utility vaults, or equipment pads in public right-of-ways.
- Retail and commercial projects: Cordon off areas during storefront renovation or parking lot repaving without a long permitting process for permanent barriers.
## Why the Sandbag Base Matters

The sandbag is doing real structural work in a panel-and-stand system. It is not a placeholder, its weight determines whether the fence stays upright in wind, withstands incidental contact from pedestrians, and remains compliant with site safety expectations.

Properly ballasted panels can handle typical Midwest weather conditions, including the wind gusts common during Indiana's spring and fall storm seasons. If a site has unusually high wind exposure, an open field, a rooftop, or a location adjacent to a highway, that is worth discussing with your rental provider before delivery so ballast weight can be adjusted.

Both systems have their place. - The fence line will need to move at least once during the project
- You are working on a surface where driven posts are not practical (asphalt, concrete, indoor surface, or a site where underground utilities prevent digging)

# Post driven temporary chain link fence

Driven into the ground. It does not lift, it does not slide, and it does not get walked through.

Post driven chain link is the temporary fence that behaves like a permanent one. Posts go into the ground and the fabric is hung on them, so the run cannot be lifted at a joint or shoved aside. What buyers ask before they order this one, including the jobs it is the wrong answer to.

Ground that will take a driven post. Rock, heavy fill and shallow utilities all change the answer, so tell us what the site is when we quote rather than after the crew arrives. Where the ground will not take a post, panels in sandbagged stands do the same job above grade.

### How long does the install take compared to panels?

Longer, and that is the honest trade. A panel run can go in the same day it is ordered on most jobs. A driven fence is a crew on site working the line, and we schedule it accordingly.

### Does it come in eight foot?

Yes. Eight foot post driven chain link is available alongside the six foot standard. Say so early and we will confirm availability with the quote.

### Can gates be set into a driven run?

Yes, pedestrian gates and drive gates both. Gate positions matter more on a driven fence than on panels, because moving one afterwards is real work rather than a two person lift.

### Does the ground get repaired when you pull it?

The posts come out with the fence at the end, and the removal is already inside the price you agreed. We are not a landscaping company and we will not pretend a driven post leaves no mark, so if the surface matters, say so and we will talk about panels instead.
