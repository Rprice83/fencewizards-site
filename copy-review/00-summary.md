# Fence Wizards website: copy review (2026-10-10)


Deep review of every page's wording (51 source files), done with the `copywriting` skill (AI-tell blacklist, clarity, headlines/CTAs) plus this project's rules (never invent facts, pricing-claim policy, keep Richard's voice). Read-only review: **nothing on the site has been changed yet.**

## The short version
- **Richard's voice is the site's biggest asset and it held up.** Trade language ("a date they can hold a sub to", "run of show", Net 30), real numbers, specific towns. The town pages' local details (US-36, Range Line Road, Grand Park, Ruoff, Mile Square…) all trace back to his original site. No pricing-policy violations anywhere (no "up to 12 months", no short-job discounts, 8 ft = special order).
- **The real problems are facts that contradict each other**, not wording. Several of them only Richard can settle (Part 1).
- **There's a lot we can fix without him** (Part 2): leftover "same day install" lines, windscreen contradictions in the blog, the cost guide's internal contradictions, 7 different button labels for the same estimator, repeated template lines on all 19 town pages, and AI-sounding patterns (mostly inherited from the original copy).

- **Windscreen:** colors, heights/sizes, what one "$800 screen" is, is 6 a firm minimum, print lead time, is fitting included, what happens at pickup.

## Part 2: Fixes we can make now (no Richard needed)

### High priority
1. **Leftover "same day install" promises** (Richard confirmed 24 to 48 hours): Greenwood FAQ "Same day is realistic here", Franklin intro "Same-day response on a straightforward run", homepage post-driven card "Set: Crew on site", homepage Emergency card "Same-week", the emergency blog headline "same-week perimeter fencing".
2. **Windscreen contradictions in the blog:** the event post says "the printed version is sold" (all windscreen is sold); the windscreen post says "we don't… sell fence material" (we sell windscreen); the post-driven post says top rail is included (it's a $1.60/ft add-on).
3. **Cost guide** (`how-much-does-temporary-fence-rental-cost`) contradicts itself after the earlier removals: implies post-driven costs more (it's cheaper per foot), says the total is "driven mostly by how long the fence is on site" (equipment-rental framing = rent clock), mixes up quote time with install time, and knocks competitors for "damage waiver fees" and "fuel surcharges" (we have a waiver and a distance surcharge). Fixable now except the parts that depend on R1/R4 and the starting-rates question.
4. **Ad landing pages:** the hero button says "Get my price" but leads to a form that gives no price; each page offers 3 different next steps; the emergency page's closing band puts the estimator ahead of the phone call; "A number for your bid, *today*" is a new promise; the event page heading "Large events in Indianapolis" sits over a list that includes Ball State, the VA and the SSA.
5. **One label for the estimator button.** Today it's 7: "Plan & Price Your Fence", "Plan & price your fence", "Plan & price it", "Price my job", "Get a Quote", "Get a Free Quote", "Draw your fence on a map". Recommendation: **"Price your fence online"** (or "Plan & price your fence" everywhere if we want the smallest change), and quote buttons say "Get my quote".

### Medium priority
6. **AI-sounding patterns** (mostly from the original copy): about 36× "rather than" across the use/fence pages (10 on the event page alone), "genuinely/honest/actually" tics, contrast reveals ("is not just X, it's Y" on Carmel, Fishers, Indianapolis, Avon, About ×3), negation lists ("No dispatch queue… No measured plan needed", About's "no rent that keeps running, no removal charge…"), em dashes in the homepage hero and stat tiles, "true and checkable rather than adjectives" used on two pages.
7. **Town pages read as one template:** identical meta description, identical FAQ intro and identical "Other towns" closing sentence on all 19; ~10 stock phrases repeated across 4–10 pages each; two pages share a hero photo (Bloomington, Lafayette). Unique meta descriptions are drafted in the appendix. The second half of the towns (Lafayette → Zionsville) never got the contraction/polish pass the first half got.
8. **Reply-time wording:** estimator intro and step 3, and the contact form, still lack "usually the same day"; the estimator intro says Richard "calls you" even when the customer picks text or email.
9. **Homepage:** hero stat tiles repeat the H1 and lede; the quick form is called "Five answers" but has no gates field; "Three generations of fencing" heading isn't supported by its body text.
10. **Blog posts:** third person ("Fence Wizards does…") where the site says "we"; "This guide covers…" openers; fake-profound endings; meta titles of 110–125 characters (Google cuts at ~60).
11. **Smaller consistency items:** five full stars shown for a 4.6 rating; "he" for estimators/buyers (use "they"); "Eighty miles" vs "80 miles"; road names "US-31" vs "US 31"; "sand bags" vs "sandbags"; stray period in the footer insurance line; British idioms on Bloomington and Columbus ("catches people out", "a river that has form"); wrong alt text on several photos (describes things that aren't visible).

### Headline ideas (homepage)
- A: "Temporary fence rental in Indianapolis, *on site in 24–48 hours.*" Puts the main search phrase back in the H1 (the original site had it).
- B: "One flat price, *fence up in 24–48 hours.*" Harder for a competitor to copy. Only once R1 is answered.
- C: "Temporary fence from the owner, *on the ground in 24–48 hours.*" Leans on Richard answering his own phone.
