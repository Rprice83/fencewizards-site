Local know-how

What's different about Zionsville.

Access is the other half. Narrow streets, brick surfaces and tight frontages mean the truck cannot always park where the fence goes, and a run has to be carried in more often than it is elsewhere. That changes the install rather than the price, but it is worth telling us before install day rather than after the truck arrives.

Away from the village it is Boone County growth: the Zionsville Road corridor, and commercial and residential development along the I-65 side of town. Those are ordinary sites with ordinary access, and they take the same 24 to 48 hour window as anything else in the radius.

Questions

Questions we get about Zionsville.

Three that come up on nearly every call from this part of the radius.

Will a fence damage a brick street or a historic frontage?

Panels sit in stands weighted with sandbags rather than in the ground, so nothing gets driven into a brick surface at all. That is the usual recommendation in the village. Where the ground would take a driven post and the perimeter has to hold for months, we will say so, but on brick the answer is panels.

Can you make the perimeter look better than bare chain link?

Yes. Windscreen fitted across the run blocks the view into the site and gives you a clean flat surface instead of open mesh. It comes plain or custom printed, so a development can put its own rendering on the run rather than showing the public a building site.

What if the truck cannot get to where the fence goes?

Tell us when you call. Narrow access and tight frontages are normal in the village, and they change how we plan the install rather than whether we take the job. What we do not want is to find out on install day with a loaded trailer on a brick street.
