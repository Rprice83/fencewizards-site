Local know-how

What's different about Bloomington.

That changes the fence. A panel run that would be fine on an industrial lot gets leaned on, sat on and pushed aside where students walk, so a lot of Bloomington work wants post-driven chain link on the pedestrian faces even when panels are enough everywhere else. The honest answer on a walk is usually a mix of the two.

The ground is the thing that actually catches people out here. Monroe County is limestone country, the terrain rolls rather than sits flat, and a post that goes in easily on a level lot in Marion County can hit rock a foot down south of town. Where that happens the answer is panels in sandbagged stands, which need no ground penetration at all. It's worth telling us what the site is before install day rather than after the crew arrives with a post driver.

Questions

Questions we get about Bloomington.

Three that come up on nearly every call from this part of the radius.

Do you work on campus-adjacent sites in Bloomington?

Yes, and it's most of what we do there. The thing to plan for is pedestrian traffic: a perimeter next to a walking route needs a driven line where people pass and can take panels everywhere else. Getting that split right is the difference between a fence that holds and one you keep calling about.

Can you hold install and strike dates set by a term calendar?

Yes. On campus-adjacent work those dates are usually fixed long before the rest of the job, so tell us both on the first call. We plan the crew and the trucks around them rather than around our own convenience.

What happens if the ground will not take a driven post?

Panels in sandbagged stands go in instead, and they do the same job above grade with no ground penetration at all. Limestone and rock are a real consideration in Monroe County, so tell us what the site is when you call and we'll quote the fence that will actually go in rather than the one that looks cheaper on paper.
